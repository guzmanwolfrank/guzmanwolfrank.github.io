Building a serverless data lake with S3, Glue and Athena

A data lake doesn't need a cluster running around the clock. With S3 for storage, Glue for cataloging and transformation, and Athena for SQL, you pay for what you use and scale without managing servers.

1. Separate your zones

I keep a landing/ prefix for raw files exactly as they arrive and a curated/ prefix for cleaned, typed Parquet. Raw data stays replayable, and analysts only touch curated.

2. Partition for how people query

s3://lake/curated/orders/year=2026/month=10/day=18/part-0001.parquet

Athena bills by data scanned. Partitioning by date and storing columnar Parquet routinely cuts scanned bytes by an order of magnitude.

3. Let Glue own the catalog

Crawlers keep table definitions in sync with S3, and PySpark Glue jobs handle the raw-to-curated transform.

4. Monitor it

CloudWatch alarms on job failures and duration route to on-call, so a broken pipeline is caught before anyone opens a dashboard.

Snowflake RBAC that scales with your team

Granting privileges straight to users works for five people and becomes unmanageable at fifty. The fix is a two-layer role hierarchy.

  • Access roles hold privileges on objects, such as read or write on a schema.
  • Functional roles map to jobs, such as analyst or engineer, and inherit access roles.
  • Users receive functional roles only. Nobody gets privileges directly.
GRANT ROLE sales_read TO ROLE analyst;
GRANT ROLE analyst    TO USER jdoe;

New schema? Create its access roles once and grant them to the right functional roles. Onboarding becomes a single grant.

What trading taught me about data pipelines

After more than fifteen years trading derivatives, I've found the habits that keep a trading system alive are the same ones that keep a data platform alive.

  • Manage drawdown. In trading you size positions to survive bad days. In pipelines you design for failure with retries, idempotent loads and replayable raw data.
  • Latency matters. A stale signal is a wrong signal, and a stale dashboard leads to wrong decisions.
  • Monitor everything. I watch a live position constantly. Production pipelines deserve the same alerting.

Both disciplines reward the same thing: systems that are boring to operate.
